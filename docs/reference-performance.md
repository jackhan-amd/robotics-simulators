AMD's official blog posts were checked again on 2026-10-29. Published performance data exists, but no comparison matched the models, units and timing scope of these supplied-example physics benchmarks. The figures below are not used to calculate speedups against MI355X physics step/s.

|---|---|---|

The GSplat article describes its summary as a 6-scene average, while its per-scene table lists 7 scenes: Bicycle, Bonsai, Counter, Garden, Kitchen, Room and Stump. Use per-scene values and the supplied benchmark script for comparisons. Do not recalculate the ambiguous average and label it an official value. Published Bicycle times are 7K 147.44 s and 30K 1102.74 s. Record differences between the original ROCm 6.4.3 stack and the stack available on MI355X.

The WAM latency measurements use MI355X, ROCm 7.14.0 and PyTorch 2.11.0. The multiple MI325X GPUs used for training and the W7900 reproduction results are separate from that latency comparison. Success rate requires closed-loop evaluation; a short latency test does not reproduce the published success rate. The [official repository](https://github.com/AMD-AGI/faster-action-models) provides FastWAM and CAE-FastWAM checkpoints and latency tools. DiT4DiT requires gated Cosmos models, and CAE-DiT4DiT checkpoints were not public.

For external context, the [nekRS performance paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/high-performance-computing/articles/10.3389/fhpcp.2024.1303358/full) compares a single MI250X GCD with V100. The Warp/JAX examples in the [MuJoCo MJX documentation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html) also differ in GPU, version and graph conditions and are not direct comparisons. The [AMD Simulation compatibility matrix](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/simulation/en/latest/about/compatibility-matrix.html) documents official support scope.
